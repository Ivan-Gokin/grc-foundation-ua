# Who sets your rules

Your requirements come from two places. The **national cybersecurity system**: the Verkhovna Rada passes the laws, the Cabinet of Ministers sets the general model, and the SSSCIP (the State Service of Special Communications and Information Protection) writes the technical rules and checks them. And your **sector regulator**, if you have one: for a bank, the National Bank of Ukraine.

To see the whole national cybersecurity system, and to tell the acts that bind you from the acts about how public authorities work, open the [map of the national cybersecurity system](#/sources?scope=core&mode=map).

Below are the bodies whose acts bind you. For each one, record:

- **its role towards you:** sets rules, inspects, receives your reports, coordinates the response to an attack;
- **the channel:** whom to contact, how, and how fast an incident must be reported.

The bodies you report incidents to (a sectoral or regional CSIRT, CERT-UA, the National Cybersecurity Coordination Centre, the SBU) mostly issue none of your acts, so they may be missing from this list. They and the reporting deadlines are in the “Legal parameter” of the incident response and communication controls (NIST CSF 2.0 RS.CO-02; ISO/IEC 27001 A.5.24–A.5.26) on step 6. Add them as separate rows on the “Regulators” sheet of the download. If no CSIRT is named there, the mapped acts of your profile do not oblige you to notify a CSIRT of cyber incidents (under Law No. 2163-VIII Art. 9¹ part 3 that duty lies with owners and administrators of systems processing state information resources, official information or state secrets, for significant cyber incidents with owners and administrators of critical information infrastructure objects, and with public authorities that learn of a cyber incident at such an object, while others may join the national system for exchanging information on cyber incidents and share it voluntarily), but the general duty of everyone to assist cybersecurity actors and tell them known data on cyber threats and cyberattacks (Art. 11) applies to you too and is shown under ISO/IEC 27001 A.5.5 and NIST CSF 2.0 RS.CO-03.

Keep these contacts in a shared place, not in one person's phone, and check them once a year.

*This is ISO/IEC 27001 control A.5.5 and NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OC-02.*
