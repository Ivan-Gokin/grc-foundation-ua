# Your Statement of Applicability

The Statement of Applicability (SoA) lists every control of your framework and says whether you apply it and why; for NIST CSF 2.0 it is called a target profile. Yours is below, built from the previous steps: a lock marks the controls you must apply, a tick the ones you choose to.

The column on the left says whether you apply each control:

- **a lock:** required by law (open the row to see which article asks for it) or by ISO/IEC 27001 clauses 4-10, which every management system needs. It cannot be unticked;
- **ticked:** recommended, because it comes from a recommended act you accepted. Untick it if it does not fit;
- **unticked:** nothing asks for it. Tick it if your own risks call for it.

Measures that the framework does not name go into the download as new rows.

Three things to watch:

- **"Partial"** means the control helps, but the law asks for more. These are the gaps most often missed.
- **Legal values**, such as how long to keep logs or how fast to report an incident: yours can be stricter, never weaker.
- **"AI, not reviewed"** mappings: check them against the text of the act before you rely on them.

Below the list are provisions that no control covers, such as reports to the regulator; they need their own lines in your documents. The download has blank columns for the owner, status and evidence of each control.
