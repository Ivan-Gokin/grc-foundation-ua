# Where the real work begins

*Covers: ISO/IEC 27001:2022, clauses 6.1.2-6.1.3, 8.2-8.3 · NIST CSF 2.0, ID.RA*

[Your baseline](#/craft?step=1) gives you the legal minimum: the controls the law requires, each with its legal basis. That is a floor, not a goal. Here the real work begins: assess the risks to your assets and decide what to add beyond the minimum. Use one risk assessment method for every management system, with different impact categories ([GRC engineering](#/guide?c=grc#how-to-integrate)).

**Goal.** Decisions driven by risk, with routine compliance kept current.

## Keeping the system current

Cybersecurity legislation in Ukraine changes several times a year, and so does the organisation, so the system needs regular review:

| Event | What to review |
|---|---|
| A new or changed act | Your laws and regulations, your SoA or profile, affected policies |
| New services, restructuring, a new supplier | Your profile and scope, roles, who sets your rules |
| A serious incident | Risks, profile, response procedures |
| Audit or regulator inspection results | Corrective actions |
| At least once a year | Everything, during management review (clause 9.3) |

**How not to miss changes.** Watch the project repository on GitHub (Watch → Custom → Releases) and check the primary sources: zakon.rada.gov.ua and cip.gov.ua.

**Common mistakes**

- the regulator's minimum treated as the maximum, ignoring the organisation's own risks;
- a separate risk method for each standard and regulator;
- the provisions no control covers, listed with your SoA, never turned into a plan with deadlines and budget.
