# I.4. Choosing the framework

**Goal.** Choose the standard whose terms you will use to describe your controls. The site supports two.

| | ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | NIST CSF 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A certifiable management system standard | A set of outcomes in six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover |
| Structure | Clauses 4-10 describe the system; Annex A lists 93 controls | Current and target profiles |
| Key document | Statement of Applicability (SoA) | Target profile |
| When to choose it | You need certification or partners ask for it | You are a CI operator or in the public sector: the baseline cyber protection measures (SSSCIP Order No. 75 of 30.01.2026) are an adapted translation of NIST CSF 2.0, so NIST CSF matches the regulator's language |

## What your sector suggests

The sector regulator often decides for you, directly or through the language of its requirements:

| Who you are | Main framework | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A bank | ISO/IEC 27001 | [NBU Board Resolution No. 95 of 28.09.2017](#/sources?act=ua-nbu-95-2017) requires a bank to implement an ISMS in line with DSTU ISO/IEC 27001 |
| A critical infrastructure operator, a public authority, the defence sector | NIST CSF 2.0 | The baseline cyber protection measures (SSSCIP Order No. 75 of 30.01.2026) are written as NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes |
| Anyone else | ISO/IEC 27001 | Certification is what customers and partners usually ask for |

The [questionnaire](#/start) suggests the framework by the same rule when you answer "Not sure".

The frameworks are not exclusive: you can certify against ISO/IEC 27001 and report a NIST CSF profile to the regulator. Pick one as the main structure and connect the other through mappings.
